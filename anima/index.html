Anima

A physical-modeling synthesizer for iPad. There are no oscillators: a source of energy, a breath or a strike, excites a resonating object, and the sound rings out into a room. Its heart is a modal resonator, so every note lands exactly in tune. Anima is the companion to Hexatone.

What it is

The instrument

Energy → object → room

No oscillators. A source of energy, a breath or a strike, excites a resonating object, and the result rings out into a room. That is the whole instrument, read left to right, cause to effect.

The object

A modal resonator

The object is a bank of 24 tuned, coupled resonances: the modes of a string, a bar, a bell. Their frequencies come straight from the note you play, with no delay lines and no lookup tables. 32 voices of polyphony.

Tuning

Every note in tune

Because pitch is computed rather than sampled, every note is exactly in tune, including any microtonal pitch an MPE controller sends. Plug in Hexatone and its per-note microtuning just works, with nothing to configure.

The surface

A panel you can read

Every control is a single bold glyph whose shape is its value, so the panel is a picture of the current sound. Load a preset and the whole surface morphs to its new shape.

The room

A room to ring into

A sixteen-line reverb and a colored echo carry every sound, from a tight space to an endless halo. Freeze holds a moment for as long as you like.

Everywhere

Standalone and AUv3

A focused standalone instrument and an AUv3 for AUM, GarageBand, Logic and Cubasis. The same engine either way, with every control automatable by the host.

In short

A companion instrument, in the same family

Anima shares Hexatone's soft, glassy pastel look and its microtonal heart, but stands on its own: a deep physical-modeling synth for iPad that earns its place in any studio. Breathy reeds, struck bells and brass, and tones no acoustic instrument could make, all from one resonating object. One price, no in-app purchases, no subscription.
